Recommendation — NO-GO until BUG-01 is fixed

The headline is genuinely good news: payment collection, approval and cancellation all work end to end in a real browser. Cancelling an approved payment restored ₹5,000 to the customer's balance, recorded who, when and why, kept the record, and wrote a timeline entry. That flow was completely broken twelve hours ago and had never once been clicked. It now works.

The headline problem is that one screen lies about money. The payment collection form told me this customer owed ₹10,000. The customer's own page, the database and every other surface said ₹25,300. A field rep standing at a counter would collect against a figure that is ₹15,300 too low. That is the exact failure mode this round existed to find, and no amount of backend testing would have caught it.

A second session then ran as a real non-Admin (Dhaval Vegad, Sales Executive), which unlocked the permission scenarios. Those results were the strongest of the round: a non-admin collected a payment successfully, the action bar rendered his exact rights, and the customer's financial fields were locked with an explanation. Two of this round's own fixes are now proven at the UI layer, not just in the database.

It found one more problem, though: data scoping is enforced inconsistently. A rep scoped to "own records" could see the entire company's collection history.

This is not a verdict on the whole product — 13 of 28 sections were still not run, including exports, responsive and credit-limit enforcement. It is a verdict on what was seen.

Findings

The payment collection screen shows the wrong outstanding balance

BUG-01 · P1 · Admin (all roles affected) · New Payment

Steps
Create a customer with a ₹10,000 opening balance. Create an order, approve it, close it. Open Payment → New Payment and select that customer.
Expected
The financial strip shows the current outstanding, ₹25,300.
Actual
It shows Outstanding ₹10,000.00 and Available Credit ₹90,000.00 — the opening balance only. The ₹15,300 closed order is ignored.
Evidence
The customer detail page, loaded moments earlier, correctly showed ₹25,300 / ₹74,700. Database confirms 10,000 + 15,300 − 0 = 25,300. Both pages were fresh loads.
Impact
A rep collects against a figure ₹15,300 too low and leaves money on the table. It is also the one screen where the number is used to make a decision in front of a customer, and the one screen that is wrong. Two screens showing different balances for the same customer destroys trust in both.
Note
Backend reconciliation passed every automated test. The engine is right; this screen's read is wrong.

"Own records only" scope is not applied to payments

BUG-12 · P1 · Sales Executive · Payments list, New Payment

Steps
Sign in as a user whose role sets global_scope: own and contacts_scope: own. Open Payments. Then open New Payment and expand the customer picker.
Expected
Only that user's own collections and own customers.
Actual
All three existing payments were listed, every one showing Collected By "sumit vegad" — another user's records, including a ₹15,900 collection. The customer picker offered every customer in the account.
Evidence
Scoping is applied elsewhere: the same user's dashboard correctly showed 0 leads and 0 converted customers where Admin saw 1 and 3. So the setting works — it is simply not enforced on the payments module.
Impact
A field rep sees the company's entire collection history: who collected how much, from whom, when. In a distribution business that is commercially sensitive between reps, and it is the exact thing "own records only" was configured to prevent. An admin who sets that scope will reasonably believe it is being honoured.

Payments module is unreachable by direct URL

BUG-02 · P2 · Admin · /payments

Steps
Navigate directly to /payments or /payments/new — typed, bookmarked, or refreshed.
Expected
The payments list loads.
Actual
Silently redirected to /dashboard. Reproduced three times. Clicking Payment in the sidebar works every time.
Impact
Bookmarks, shared links and browser refresh all dump the user on the dashboard with no explanation. Finance lives on this page all day; a refresh losing their place is a daily irritation, and a shared payment link is unusable.

Outstanding column in the customer list has no currency formatting

BUG-03 · P2 · Customer list

Expected
₹10,000, consistent with every other screen.
Actual
Raw 10000 and 0, no symbol, no separators — in the single column a rep scans to answer "who owes me money?"
Impact
At six or seven figures this becomes genuinely hard to read at a glance, and it is inconsistent with the dashboard, order list and financial cards, all of which format correctly.

"Contact Person" column displays the company name

BUG-04 · P2 · Customer list

Expected
Rakesh Shah — the contact person entered.
Actual
ABC Distributors — the company. The customer detail page separates the two correctly, so the list is mislabelled or bound to the wrong field.
Impact
A rep looking for who to ask for at the shop sees the shop's name instead. The data is captured correctly; only the list is wrong.

Closed and Cancelled orders are both red

BUG-05 · P2 · Order list

Actual
Pending is amber, Approved is green, and Closed and Cancelled are the same red.
Impact
Closed is a successful outcome — goods delivered, revenue booked, the only status that counts toward outstanding. Cancelled is the opposite. Colouring them identically means the list cannot be scanned for business health, and Closed reading as "bad" is actively misleading.

Duplicate products with identical SKUs in the order picker

BUG-06 · P2 · New Order

Actual
Dummy Product 10 (DUMMY-0010) and Dummy Product 11 (DUMMY-0011) each appear twice, same name, same SKU.
Impact
A rep cannot tell them apart, and the two copies may carry different prices or tax settings. Sales reporting by product silently splits across duplicates. Likely seed data, but it is in the live catalogue.

The financial summary does not show its own workings

BUG-07 · UX · Customer detail

Actual
The card shows Outstanding ₹10,000, Total Closed Orders ₹0, Paid ₹0. Those three numbers contradict each other — unless you know there is a ₹10,000 opening balance, which appears nowhere on the card.
Impact
Section 26 asks whether Finance would trust this screen enough to make a decision. As shown, a finance user cannot reconcile it without opening the edit form. Adding Opening Balance as a fourth figure would make the card self-explanatory.

"Healthy" for a customer who owes money and has never paid

BUG-08 · UX · Customer detail, New Order, New Payment

Actual
ABC Distributors owed ₹25,300 with ₹0 ever collected, and Payment Status read a green Healthy.
Impact
Technically correct — nothing is past its credit days yet — but "Healthy" is a judgement, not a fact, and it is the most prominent word on the card. "Within terms" would say the same thing without implying the relationship is fine.

Two different number formats for the same currency

BUG-09 · P3 · Across screens

Actual
Customer card: ₹100,000 (international grouping). Payment form: ₹1,00,000.00 (Indian grouping, two decimals). Order list: ₹15,300 (no decimals).
Impact
Cosmetic individually; together they make the product feel assembled rather than designed, and mixed grouping is a real misreading risk at scale.

Order line total does not equal quantity × price

BUG-10 · UX · Order detail

Actual
The Details tab shows Qty 100, Price ₹150, Total ₹15,300 — with no tax line. The ₹300 of IGST is only visible during creation.
Impact
Anyone checking the arithmetic gets 15,000 and concludes the order is wrong. The creation screen breaks tax out properly; the detail view should too.

A customer in credit shows a negative balance and a green "Healthy"

BUG-13 · UX · Customer detail

Actual
Balaji Supermarket has a ₹4,500 approved payment and no closed orders, so the card reads Outstanding Balance −₹4,500, Payment Status Healthy, Total Closed Orders ₹0, Paid ₹4,500.
Impact
The arithmetic is right — the customer is in credit — but nothing on screen says so. A rep sees a negative number next to a green tick and cannot tell whether it is good, bad, or a bug. This is the contradiction test from section 18, met organically: "Advance / credit balance ₹4,500" would communicate the same fact honestly.

Financial inputs carry no currency affordance

BUG-11 · P3 · Customer form

Actual
Credit Limit, Credit Days and Opening Balance are bare number boxes. Credit Limit and Opening Balance both show placeholder 0.00 with no ₹ prefix; Credit Days sits between them in the same style but is a day count, not money.
Impact
Easy to type a rupee value into Credit Days, or to mistake the scale of a limit. A ₹ prefix on the two money fields and a "days" suffix on the third removes the ambiguity.

CTO questions, on the evidence seen

QuestionAnswer
Business correctnessThe order and payment lifecycles behave the way a distribution business expects. Outstanding moves only on Closed, and only on Approved money.
UX for a non-accountantMixed. The flows are clear and the dialogs explain consequences well. The vocabulary — "Outstanding (Calculated)", "Healthy", a card whose figures do not visibly reconcile — assumes more than a rep has.
Can a user corrupt financial data?Not through the Admin paths exercised. Every destructive action asked for confirmation, and cancellation demanded a reason.
Is the user seeing what they shouldn't?Unknown. Not testable in an Admin-only session.
ConsistencyNo. BUG-01 is a direct contradiction between two screens about the same customer's debt, plus three currency formats.
Would Finance trust this screen?The payment detail and timeline, yes — clear, complete, auditable. The customer financial card, not yet: its own numbers do not add up on screen.
Can a rep collect quickly?Yes. Customer → New Payment → amount → type → Save is four interactions with sensible defaults. But BUG-01 means the figure they see while doing it is wrong.

Recommended actions

  1. Fix BUG-01 before anyone collects money. Everything else here can ship with a known issue; this one causes under-collection at the counter.
  2. Fix BUG-02. Small, and it removes a daily annoyance for the heaviest user of the module.
  3. Add Opening Balance to the financial card and take Closed out of the red status colour. Two small changes that make the finance screens trustworthy at a glance.
  4. Run the sections that were not executed — especially exports, which have now gone unverified three rounds running, and every permission scenario, which needs the four logins created.
  5. Then re-run this round with a Field Rep session, which is where the permission-hiding and unauthorised-action findings actually live.
